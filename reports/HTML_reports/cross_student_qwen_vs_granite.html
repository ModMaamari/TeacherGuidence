Experiment report · exp_cross_student · 2026-07-16

A student learns as well from another student's traces — and inherits its habits

Qwen3.5-0.8B was fine-tuned twice on MiniMax-M3 teacher-guidance traces over the same 3,000 HotpotQA questions: once on traces where Qwen itself was the acting student, once on traces from the stronger Granite-4.1-3B. Semantic accuracy came out identical — but the granite-trained model answers more precisely, stops earlier, and runs 22% faster.

1 · Design

One model, two data authors, everything else pinned

The only variable is which student produced the training trajectories. Teacher (MiniMax-M3), prompts, budgets (3 planning rounds + 3 hidden running steps), SFT recipe (LoRA r32/α64, lr 1e-4, 2 epochs, effective batch 16, completion-only loss), and evaluation harness are identical across both arms.

Traces2 × 3,000 episodes on the same questions: Qwen-as-student (57.6% correct), Granite-as-student (70.9%)
DatasetsCorrect episodes → guidance-as-internal-thought SFT examples (5,808 / 6,712)
TrainingQwen3.5-0.8B × 2, same hyperparameters, 4×A100, ~15 min each
Eval3 models × 2 test sets × 3 seeds × 200 questions, teacherless, budget 3 hidden
Judge3,600 verdicts by gpt-oss-120b (independent of the data-generating teacher)
Contamination control. Both runs cover the same 3,000 questions and each trained model is evaluated on both test sets, so a test question could otherwise appear in training via the other run. The union of all 386 test qids was banned from both training sets; zero overlap was verified.

4 · Accuracy

Semantically, a dead heat

The judge (which sees only question, gold answer, and the model's answer) scores both trained models the same on both test sets. Whose trajectories the data came from does not change how often the student is right — only training at all does, lifting a near-useless base model by ~45 points.

5 · Precision

Where the trace author shows: answer form

Cover-match asks "does the answer contain the gold string"; exact-match and F1 reward saying it precisely. The self-trained model wraps correct answers in verbose sentences — Qwen's own style in the traces. The granite-trained model learned granite's habit of answering in the gold answer's exact form: 3.6× the exact-match, +14 F1 points, at identical semantic accuracy.

6 · Efficiency

Granite's discipline came with the data

In the source runs, granite found short paths far more often than qwen (22% vs 6% two-step episodes). That behaviour transferred: the cross-trained model finishes voluntarily 2.4× as often, takes fewer steps, emits ~10% fewer tokens, produced zero invalid actions across all 3,600 episodes — and its eval runs finish 22% sooner.

7 · Verdict

Trace quality beats trace ownership

  1. There is no self-data advantage. Training a 0.8B student on trajectories produced by a different (larger) student costs nothing in semantic accuracy — the judge scores the two arms within 0.5 points on both test sets.
  2. Behavioural quality transfers from the trace author. The granite-trained model inherited granite's precision (EM ×3.6, F1 +14pts), its early stopping (2.4× voluntary finishes), and its format discipline (0 invalid actions vs 19) — visible even though accuracy is tied.
  3. Training is the story; provenance is the footnote. Both arms lift the base model from ~7% to ~50% judge-correct. If a stronger student's traces are available, prefer them — the same teacher spend yields more correct episodes (+16% data here) and better habits.

Caveats

The granite dataset is 16% larger because a stronger student produces more correct episodes — data source and quantity are not fully separable in this design (that coupling is also what "use the stronger student's data" means in practice). One student pair, one dataset (HotpotQA), one size (0.8B); 3 seeds per cell. EM/F1 differences are far outside seed noise; the judge tie is robust to it.
